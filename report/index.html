Adaptive-Step DSRL

DSRL steers a frozen diffusion policy by having an RL agent choose the starting noise of the sampler. We test whether steering later in the denoising chain (at step k) works better, and whether a policy that picks k for each action chunk beats any fixed choice. The base policies are DPPO robomimic checkpoints on can, square and transport.

What we know so far (updated Nov 6; transport still training toward 6M steps)

  1. Transport had not converged at 3M steps, so we are extending it to 6M. Steps 3 and 5 learn faster than DSRL (k=0), but DSRL is catching up: at 4.8M, steps 0, 3 and 5 are at 0.77, 0.75 and 0.77 (mean of the last 3 evals, one seed). Steps 8 and 11 keep rising (0.67, 0.59); steps 14–19 stay at 0.17–0.35, near the base policy's 0.195. Every transport run first dropped to 1–10% success around 0.9–1.4M env steps and recovered after about 1.6M.
  2. On transport, random step choice matches the best fixed step; learned choice does not yet. At 4.0M steps, where every run can be compared, random k averages 0.70 (0.63, 0.77, 0.70 over three seeds) and the best fixed step 0.77, while adaptive averages 0.61 (0.49, 0.76, 0.59), the same as DSRL (0.61). Adaptive and random k reach 6M on Nov 7.
  3. Square saturates like can. Steps 0–6, adaptive (0.96, three seeds) and random k (0.96) all end at 0.93–0.97; only step 7 lags at 0.81. Can seeds 2–3 confirm seed 1: adaptive 0.92 and 0.94, random k 0.91 and 0.93.
  4. A wider noise bound gives later steps back their success. In the trained sweep on can (Bound b), step 4 rises from 0.90 to 0.97 at ±2, step 6 from 0.84 to 0.92 at ±3, and step 7 from 0.77 to 0.85 at ±3. Step 0 gains nothing and collapses to 0 at ±3. So each step has its own best bound; one seed.
  5. Every steering method saturates on can. All runs at steps 0–4 and both adaptive runs end at 0.90–0.96 success, against 0.42 for the base policy. Differences among them are about the noise of a single eval (±1.5 points), so can does not separate the methods. Square and transport are the real test.
  6. Late steps end lower. Steps 5, 6 and 7 end at 0.88, 0.84 and 0.77. They move the action least (Fig 3a), and a policy that can only nudge the base action gets less out of RL.
  7. Steering at a late step helps even without RL. Restarting denoising from the model's clean-action estimate with zero added noise scores 0.81 at step 1 on can, with no training. So part of each step's advantage comes from the change of sampler. The RL gain at step k should be measured against that step's no-RL score, not the base policy. The same holds at step 0: feeding the base policy smaller, bounded noise (tanh of a Gaussian, the shape DSRL's untrained actor outputs) raises it from 0.42 to 0.71 without RL. This base policy simply does better with less noise.
  8. Re-checked base success on can: 0.421 ± 0.013 (1,500 episodes, 3 seeds). The earlier 0.375 came from 200 episodes and was low by sampling noise. Figures and text now use 0.421.
  9. Adaptive gets close to the best fixed step without a sweep, but random step choice is almost as good (0.93 vs 0.94). On can, learning which step to use adds at most a couple of points beyond mixing steps at random.
  10. Two baselines are weak. Residual RL in action space ends at about 0.5 on can and fails on square (0.01–0.10) and transport (0.00); it starts near zero there and is likely under-tuned. Searching for one fixed noise vector reaches only 0.09, because this base policy needs fresh noise for each action chunk.

No training

Steering with zero noise, per step

Success of the frozen base policy when denoising is restarted at step k from the model's clean-action estimate with zero added noise. 200 episodes for can and square, 600 for transport (SE about 0.035 and 0.018). Zero noise at step 0 means one constant starting noise, which fails on every task. At later steps it beats the base policy on can and transport, but not on square at steps 1–3.

Caveat check

Does a wider noise bound give late steps back their control?

The policy's noise is bounded at ±1 per dimension at every step, a setting inherited from DSRL and never tuned per step. The figure below measures control (the spread of the final action over random noise, 500 dataset states) with the bound widened to ±1.5, ±2 and ±3. Filled markers match or exceed the control that step 0 has at the current ±1 bound; hollow markers fall short. On can and square, middle steps catch up at ±1.5–2. The last step comes closest on can at ±3 (0.083 against 0.084) and stays well short on square and transport. A wider bound also pushes the partly denoised action further from anything the model saw in training, so more control does not have to mean better actions. No training was run; this is a measurement only.

We then trained steps 0, 4, 6 and 7 on can at each wider bound (one seed, 1M env steps). The result is below.

Follow-ups on transport (exp13)

Headroom for a learned step, the early dip, and equal control strength

  • A learned step choice has little room to help. Using the critic of the uniform-random-k agent (trained to 6M), picking the best step and noise per chunk out of 20 steps × 32 noises scores 0.878. Picking the best of 640 noises at k=0, with the same number of critic calls, scores 0.873 (paired test p = 0.83, 600 episodes per arm). Both beat the agent's own actor (0.825–0.828). The step the critic prefers for a given state also did not repeat across seeds.
  • None of the three fixes removes the early dip. All arms drop in success during the first 64k learning steps after the warm start, at k=0 and k=3 alike. The noise trust region recovers slightly earlier than the baseline, but stays within the noise of three seeds. The kick parameterization with a zero-initialised actor starts exactly at the base policy (0.19 at k=0) and still collapses, and it recovers slowest. The runs were stopped at 1.66M once the comparison was clear.
  • Equal control strength (one seed). Later steps move the action less, mainly the gripper. These runs scale the noise per action dimension so that steps 3, 5 and 8 move the action as much as step 0 does. At step 8 this changes the picture: the run reached 0.87 by 4.2M, while k=0 and standard-noise k=8 were at 0.61 and 0.53 at 4.0M. At 9M, the mean of the last 3 evals is 0.85 at step 8 (standard noise stopped at 0.82 at 6M), 0.88 at step 3 (standard 0.91) and 0.91 at step 5 (standard 0.90). So part of what makes step 8 worse is lost control strength rather than the step itself. This is one seed. Adaptive k at equal strength is still training (0.93 at 7.0M, level with the standard-noise run) and picks steps 0–5 in two thirds of chunks.
Critical windows (exp14)

Where in denoising does the policy decide?

Without any RL, we measured at which DDIM step the frozen transport policy commits to each part of its action. It makes its discrete choices (which kind of action, whether to open or close a gripper) in a short stretch around step 5–6 of 20, and that stretch is in the same place in every task phase. In the simulator, changing one chunk at any step left success unchanged, and the trained critic could not tell good chunks from bad ones within a state. So a per-chunk choice of step has nothing to exploit; we recommend steering at one fixed early step (k ≈ 3–8).
